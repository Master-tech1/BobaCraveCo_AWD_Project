Our story

Tea first. Pearls second. Patience always.

Long before bubble tea had a name, tea was a craft of patience: leaves picked by hand at first light, water brought to a slow simmer over a wood fire, and starch from the cassava root rolled between palms into small, dark pearls.

Boba Crave Co. began with one question: why does most bubble tea taste like sugar and nothing else? We went back to the old way, and we kept its rules. Real leaves, fresh milk, and tapioca cooked by hand until it turns glossy and tender.

  1. Centuries ago · The hills

    The leaf

    Tea in these hills was picked by hand, never by the sheet: the youngest leaves and the bud, taken at dawn while the dew was still on them. They were withered in the open air, rolled by palm, and fired slowly, a craft that families handed from one season to the next.

  2. Before the stove · The kettle

    The fire

    Water was kept at a slow, steady simmer in heavy kettles over a wood fire, tended by eye and by ear. The fire could not be hurried, so the tea could not be either. That lesson, slow and even heat, is the one we kept.

  3. Every household · The pearl

    The roll

    Cassava root was ground to a fine starch, kneaded into a warm dough, and rolled into pearls by hand, each one pinched and shaped, then simmered until it turned translucent and chewy to the very centre.

  4. Singapore, 1980s · The tea stall

    The shake

    In busy Singaporean tea stalls, vendors began shaking cold tea to order and dropping chewy tapioca pearls into the cup. A street-side idea that people queued for, and that travelled the world.

  5. Today · Boba Crave Co.

    Modern perfection

    We bring that old patience to a modern kitchen. Pearls are simmered in small batches, with a fresh pot started every hour, and no pearl waits more than two hours. We use real leaves, fresh milk, brown sugar cooked down in-house and crystal-clear ice. No shortcuts, no powders, and no pearl served past its best.

The brewing craft

Steep

Whole leaves in water held just below boiling, timed to the minute for each tea.

Simmer

Dry tapioca simmers, rests, then soaks in syrup until chewy to the center.

Chill

Tea cools naturally so the flavor stays round, never bitter or flat.

Shake

Each cup is shaken to order with ice, so it arrives cold, frothy, and balanced.
